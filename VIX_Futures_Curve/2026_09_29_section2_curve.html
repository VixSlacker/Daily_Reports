How to read this chart

Each line is one session's constant-maturity curve: the settlement curve interpolated to fixed maturities of 30, 60, 90, 120 and 150 calendar days.

Fixed maturities are what make the sessions comparable. Real contracts lose a day of life every session, so raw contract curves shift sideways underneath you and shape change gets confused with the passage of time.

The buttons are independent, so any combination of prior sessions can sit on the chart at once. The scale is computed from all five and does not move.

Shape is read from the gaps between adjacent maturities. A curve in contango normally decays: each 30-day step rises less than the one before it. Departures from that decay are what the knee and kick wording marks, and on most sessions there is nothing to mark.

About the VIX and basis columns
The table lists each session's constant-maturity levels, then VIX and the basis. The basis is VIX minus CM30, in volatility points: VIX is the spot index and CM30 an interpolated futures price, so the gap is spot measured against the front of the futures curve, not two readings of one thing. It is usually negative, spot below the curve; it turns positive when spot jumps above the front, as it does in a spike.
Constant-maturity levels are interpolated from standardised monthly VX futures settlements to fixed maturities of 30, 60, 90, 120 and 150 calendar days. Sessions shown: Wed 23 Sep to Tue 29 Sep.
